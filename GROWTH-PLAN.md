## 1. Earn through client services first

Allocate approximately 60% of available business-development time to sales, 25% to delivering useful content and case studies, and 15% to product interviews. Limit product monetization work to one active experiment at a time.

### One clear offer

Start with small training centers that currently collect inquiries through Facebook and WhatsApp. Interview five owners before committing to this niche. If another niche already supplies warm contacts, test that niche first instead.

Offer: an Arabic mobile-friendly course landing page with course details, a clear inquiry action, a lead form, basic conversion measurement, and an agreed delivery date. Define who supplies copy and images, revision limits, and ongoing costs in the quote.

Use the existing apps as evidence of design and development capability. Explain their actual stage and demonstrate working features; do not imply client results or traction that do not exist.

Sell three bounded packages:

| Package | Deliverable | Commercial model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Launch | One landing page and inquiry flow | Fixed project fee |
| Business | Small website plus a defined booking or lead workflow | Fixed project fee after scoping |
| Care | Updates, monitoring, backups where applicable, and a monthly lead report | Monthly fee with explicit limits |

Set prices from estimated hours, your minimum acceptable rate, third-party expenses, and contingency. Quote hosting/domain costs separately or state exactly what is included. Obtain a deposit before production. Avoid unlimited maintenance promises.

### Change the sales journey

The current homepage invites people to discover projects and email the studio. Add a specific service offer, examples, a short inquiry form or WhatsApp action, a clear next step, and two honest project case studies. Each case study should show the problem, your contribution, screenshots, and only measured outcomes. These are proposed changes; this plan does not modify the site.

Track: source -> inquiry -> qualified conversation -> proposal -> deposit -> completed delivery -> maintenance renewal. Record the prospect's business, contact route, problem, last contact, next action, expected value, and status.

### Weekly acquisition routine

- Research 25 businesses that fit the chosen niche and have a visible website or inquiry-flow problem.
- Prepare 10 personalized approaches per week. Each should identify one real problem and offer a small next step.
- Follow up once after roughly four business days and once the following week when appropriate. Stop when someone declines.
- Publish three SW Studio posts: one practical tip, one demonstration or case study, and one clear service offer.
- Hold at least two discovery conversations when pipeline volume allows. Ask about the business problem, current process, budget range, decision maker, and timing.
- Ask satisfied clients for a testimonial and an introduction to one relevant business.

Outreach draft, to adapt and send manually:

> أهلاً، أنا أحمد من SW Studio. لاحظت إن تفاصيل الكورسات عندكم موزعة بين البوستات، وده ممكن يصعّب وصول العميل للمعلومة. ممكن أشارككم مثال بسيط لصفحة تجمع التفاصيل وتسهل الاستفسار على واتساب؟

No outreach has been sent as part of this plan.

### 90-day sequence

| Period | Actions | Decision / output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–7 | Interview five owners; choose niche; define offer and scope; prepare two honest case studies; create lead tracker | One clear offer and a prospect list |
| Days 8–30 | Run weekly acquisition routine; capture objections; publish demonstrations; connect the two Facebook Pages to the chosen scheduler | Aim for 40 tailored approaches, 4–6 qualified conversations, 2 proposals, and 1 paid project |
| Days 31–60 | Deliver, measure actual inquiry behavior, collect permission for a case study, offer bounded care plan, interview users of one app | Repeatable delivery checklist and first recurring service offer |
| Days 61–90 | Repeat the best-performing acquisition source; test one paid product offer; consider a small, capped ad experiment only after organic inquiries convert | Evidence for where to invest next |

Those funnel numbers are working targets. If the first 20 tailored approaches produce no useful replies, revise the audience, offer, or opening message. If conversations happen but proposals do not, improve qualification. If proposals repeatedly fail, investigate trust, scope, timing, and price. Review collected revenue and delivery margin, not just follower counts.

## 3. Facebook publishing without Metricool

### Recommended starting option: Buffer Free

Buffer currently supports multiple Facebook Pages. Its free plan allows three channels, with ten queued posts per channel at once. SW Studio and Infaq would use two channels, leaving one spare. Each Facebook Page counts as a channel. Published posts free queue slots; the ten-post limit is not a monthly allowance.

At three posts per week for each Page, a two-week batch needs six queue slots per Page, within that limit. This automates publishing after preparation; it does not independently produce a fresh editorial calendar forever.

Setup checklist:

1. Sign in to Buffer and choose its Free plan.
2. Connect SW Studio through the Facebook account with the necessary Page publishing access.
3. Connect Infaq as a second channel if access is available. Confirm both Page identities before queuing anything.
4. Set scheduling to Africa/Cairo. Trial slots: Sunday, Tuesday, Thursday for SW Studio; Saturday, Monday, Wednesday for Infaq, at 7 pm local time. Adjust using results; these are trial times, not evidence-based best times.
5. Prepare six approved posts per Page. Start with text, links, and ordinary image posts whose automatic publishing support is confirmed in the composer.
6. Schedule one test post per Page, verify successful publication, then queue the rest.
7. Refill weekly and check publishing failures and reconnect notices.

No Page has been connected and no post has been scheduled by this planning task. Page authorization and the final content are still required for activation.

### If automatic content intake is needed: Make

Use a content queue feeding Make's Facebook Pages integration. Make documents post and photo-post creation and a Facebook connection flow. Start with its standard connection; a custom Meta application is a separate implementation choice.

Suggested workflow:

1. Run on an agreed schedule, initially three times daily rather than constantly polling.
2. Read due queue items: status=approved, publish time reached, valid Page identifier, and no stored Facebook post ID.
3. Process each due item and route it to SW Studio or Infaq using a validated Page mapping.
4. Publish the text/link or supported photo format.
5. Store the returned post ID, publication time, and published status.
6. On an explicit failure, record the error and flag it for review. On a timeout with an uncertain outcome, reconcile against Facebook before retrying to avoid duplicate posts.

Queue fields: content_id, page_key, caption, image_url, destination_url, publish_at, timezone, status, facebook_post_id, published_at, error.

Use page-specific copy and destination links. AI can help create drafts, with a deliberate approval step before items become publishable. Never invent donation opportunities, beneficiary stories, totals, verification claims, or testimonials. Store authentication in the automation provider's credential system, not the content queue.

Make charges by credits consumed by scenario actions. Check the current allowance and actual test-run usage before choosing a plan. Empty polling runs can consume allowance too. For illustration, two Pages at three posts a week produce approximately 26 posts in a 30-day month, plus polling, queue updates, and any AI calls; post count alone does not determine cost.

For more infrastructure control later, n8n has a self-hosted Community Edition and a Facebook Graph API node. Hosting, maintenance, Page authorization, and possibly Meta app/access review remain work to account for. Use this when its flexibility justifies that work.
